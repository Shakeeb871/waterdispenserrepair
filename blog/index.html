How to Clean a Water Dispenser: Step-by-Step Guide for UAE Homes and Offices

Home / Blog

Switch the dispenser off, drain it, wash the tank and the removable parts, then run the approved cleaning solution through the taps. Rinse until no trace of the cleaner is left, refit everything dry, and restart the machine once the tank has refilled.

That is the basic sequence for many dispensers, but the exact procedure varies by model. Start with the model documentation. It should specify the cleaning product, dilution, contact time and flushing procedure. Tank access, drain points and removable parts also vary between machines.

Before You Clean: Identify Your Water Dispenser Type

A useful way to group dispensers is by where the water comes from and how you get to it.

Top-Load Bottled Dispensers

The bottle sits inverted on top of the cabinet. Many top-load designs have an internal tank you can reach from above once the bottle is lifted off. Models with a hot-water function may have a separate hot-water tank; check the model documentation before opening any panel.

Bottom-Load Bottled Dispensers

The bottle stands inside the cabinet and a pump lifts water to the dispensing point. Access and cleaning methods vary between models; some procedures use the pump and water lines. Bottom-load designs vary considerably between brands.

Plumbed-In Dispensers

These draw from a supply line rather than a bottle. A plumbed-in unit may contain filters, UV treatment or an internal tank, so use its own service procedure rather than a bottled-dispenser routine.

Cleaning, Sanitising, Descaling and Repair: What’s the Difference?

These tasks are often grouped under the single word “cleaning”, but they address different problems.

  • Cleaning removes dirt and residue. Washing the drip tray in soapy water is cleaning.
  • Sanitising reduces microorganisms. It uses the manufacturer-approved product and contact time specified for the machine.
  • Descaling removes mineral deposits using the method specified for the machine.
  • Maintenance replaces consumables on a schedule. Fitting a new carbon cartridge is maintenance.
  • Repair corrects a fault. Replacing a failed thermostat is repair.

A thorough clean does not test the cooling circuit, the heater, the pump or the wiring. Those need diagnosis.

How Often Should You Clean a Water Dispenser?

Start with the manufacturer’s cleaning interval, then check any requirements that apply to the premises.

Dubai Municipality’s Technical Guidelines for Quality of Unbottled Drinking Water (DM-HSD-GU17-DW2, September 2024) covers drinking-water coolers for premises within the scope of that guidance. Section 10-2 includes manufacturer-directed cleaning, plus cleaning and disinfection once every six months along with the building’s water systems, and draining and re-cleaning after a week or more of downtime. These are requirements for in-scope premises, not a cleaning rule for every private home in the UAE.

A dispenser running continuously in a busy office needs closer attention to the drip tray, the taps and filter condition than one in a family kitchen.

What You Need Before You Start

  • The model manual or the manufacturer’s cleaning instructions
  • The cleaning or sanitising product approved for your machine
  • A long-handled brush with soft bristles, where the procedure calls for one
  • Washing-up liquid and clean cloths
  • A bucket
  • Rubber gloves

Do not mix bleach with vinegar, acids, ammonia or any other cleaning product, because the combination can release harmful gases.

Use only a product the manufacturer approves for the inside of the machine. A dilution intended for a kitchen surface should not be assumed to be suitable for a water system. A product suitable for one dispenser may not be approved for another model. If no cleaning method is listed anywhere in the documentation, ask the manufacturer or your supplier before pouring anything in.

How to Clean a Top-Load Bottled Water Dispenser

The sequence below suits many top-load machines. Skip or adapt any step your model does not have.

Step 1: Switch Off the Power and Hot-Water Function

Unplug the dispenser at the socket. If it has a hot tap, switch off the hot-water function as well. Some models have a separate hot-water switch, so use the controls shown in the model instructions.

Keep the hot-water function off until the hot tank has refilled, following the manufacturer’s safety instructions. Let the hot water cool before you drain it.

Step 2: Drain the Dispenser

Lift the bottle off and open the taps into a bucket. Some machines empty completely through the taps. Others have a separate drain point for the hot tank. Do not force a stiff drain cap with a tool; follow the model instructions for opening and closing it.

Step 3: Clean Accessible Removable Parts

Take out the drip tray first, then any baffle or probe the documentation lists as removable. Not every machine has all three. Wash them in warm soapy water, rinse them, and leave them to dry fully before they go back.

Step 4: Prepare the Approved Cleaning Solution

Mix only as much solution as the procedure calls for, at the dilution given for your machine.

Step 5: Clean the Tank and Accessible Water Paths

Brush the walls and floor of the tank, then the seat where the bottle rests. Avoid abrasive pads that can scratch the tank surface. Hold the solution for the stated contact time and no longer.

Step 6: Flush the Taps

Drain the solution out through the taps instead of tipping it from the tank. On designs that circulate the solution through the taps, this carries it through the tubing and tap bodies. It will not reach a sealed section of the water path.

Step 7: Rinse Thoroughly

Follow the model’s flushing sequence rather than a fixed number of rinses. Then check for any smell or taste of the cleaner before the dispenser goes back into drinking use.

Step 8: Reassemble and Restart

Refit the dry parts, load a fresh bottle and plug the machine in. Switch the hot-water function back on once that tank has refilled. Give it time to heat and chill before judging either tap.

How to Clean a Bottom-Load Water Dispenser

Treat what follows as a general route rather than a procedure for your machine. Designs vary considerably between brands. The pump and probe assembly can be damaged if parts are removed incorrectly.

Isolate the machine and take the bottle out. Drain through the taps into a bucket, then wash the drip tray and any removable spout cover in soapy water.

Where the model permits this method, the cleaning solution is drawn in by standing the pump probe in a container of solution. Running the taps pulls it through the system, and clean water then follows the same route to rinse. Other machines use a supplied cleaning kit or a dedicated port instead, and some require the probe assembly to stay where it is.

Check the documentation before detaching anything attached to the pump. A damaged probe seal can turn routine cleaning into a repair.

How to Clean a Plumbed-In Water Dispenser

Shut the inlet valve and unplug the unit before anything else. Wipe the touch panel, the nozzles and the cabinet with a food-safe cloth. Take the drip tray out, wash it, and dry it before refitting.

Filter condition is an important maintenance point on these machines. The interval and the flushing sequence belong to the cartridge rather than to any general rule.

Fit the new cartridge in the position shown, then restore the water and power. Flush as the filter manufacturer instructs. Some new carbon cartridges release fine carbon particles during initial flushing, so follow the cartridge manufacturer’s flushing instructions before drinking the water.

Where a UV lamp is fitted, the replacement interval is set by the model. A lamp may continue to light after its effective output has fallen, so follow that interval rather than waiting for it to fail.

Where a unit has a sealed water path, sanitising it is a service task requiring the manufacturer’s kit or an engineer.

How to Descale a Hot-Water Tank

Descaling removes mineral deposits. It is a separate task from cleaning and sanitising, and it does not replace either of them.

Heating encourages mineral deposits to build up on hot-water parts. How quickly that happens depends on the water supply and how heavily the hot tap is used.

Watch for the hot tap running slower than the cold, water arriving warm rather than hot, or a ticking sound from the cabinet while the element heats. Each of those can have other causes, so check them before assuming scale is the problem.

Descale only where the manufacturer permits it and only with the product they name. Vinegar and citric acid are both used as descalers, but neither suits every hot tank, and some manufacturers do not allow owner descaling at all.

Where it is permitted, the sequence runs: switch off the heating, introduce the solution, then draw it through by running the hot tap. Hold it for the stated time, then drain and rinse. On designs where deposits lie beyond the upper tank, the specified circulation path is important.

Common Water Dispenser Cleaning Mistakes

  • Guessing at a dilution instead of measuring the one specified.
  • Mixing cleaning products, in particular bleach with vinegar, acids or ammonia.
  • Taking an abrasive pad to tank surfaces.
  • Leaving a solution in the machine past its contact time.
  • Counting rinses instead of running the flushing sequence.
  • Detaching pump parts or seals that are not listed as user-removable.
  • Powering the hot-water function before the tank has refilled.
  • Refitting parts while they are still damp.

If nothing comes out after the bottle goes back on, reseat the bottle and check whether the dispenser draws water normally. Reseating the bottle can clear an air lock on some machines. Where the flow is still dead afterwards, the cause lies somewhere else and the unit needs inspecting.

How to Keep Your Water Dispenser Clean Between Deep Cleans

Position the machine away from direct sunlight and away from heat sources. Store spare bottles away from direct sunlight and heat rather than on a balcony or in a corridor.

Wipe the neck and cap of each new bottle before loading it. Follow the storage and use period stated on the bottle or by the water supplier, instead of leaving a part-used one on the machine for weeks.

Empty and dry the drip tray, and keep the floor around the machine dry. Section 10-2 of the cited guideline asks for the floors around coolers to be dried, to prevent the breeding of insects and the gathering of algae around the radiator.

Keep tea, juice and soft drinks away from the dispensing area. Sugary residue can remain around the taps and make cleaning harder.

If your model has condenser coils or vents you can get to, keep them clear and leave space around the cabinet for heat to escape. Check vents and accessible coils regularly, especially in dusty environments.

Once the machine has stood unused for a week or more, follow the model’s recommissioning or cleaning procedure before it is used again.

Signs Your Water Dispenser Needs Cleaning

These signs can indicate contamination or deposits, and should prompt inspection and the cleaning procedure recommended for the model:

  • Taste or smell turning stale, metallic or chemical
  • Cloudy water, or particles appearing in the glass
  • Green or dark patches at the taps, on removable parts or in the drip tray
  • A slippery film on surfaces you can touch

Two further symptoms are mechanical, and they belong in the troubleshooting sections below rather than here:

  • One tap flowing more slowly than another
  • Water that no longer chills or heats properly

Section 10-2 of the cited guidance covers this for premises within its scope. It asks for a cooler showing signs of contamination, or a change in taste or smell, to be shut down from service until it has been cleaned and disinfected.

Once biofilm has formed, rinsing alone may not remove it, and brushing followed by the approved cleaning or sanitising procedure may be needed. Heavy mineral scale is a separate problem that can restrict flow and reduce how well hot-water parts perform.

When You Should Stop Cleaning and Call a Professional

Stop cleaning and arrange a service if any of these appear:

  • A burning smell, or heat damage around the wiring or the plug
  • The socket, breaker or safety switch tripping repeatedly
  • Water reaching wiring or electrical parts
  • Damaged, brittle or exposed cable
  • Growth in an area you cannot reach, or growth returning soon after cleaning
  • A cracked tank
  • A pump that runs without delivering water, or that does not run at all
  • The cabinet or compressor area running unusually hot
  • A suspected fault in the sealed cooling system

Electrical, sealed-system and damaged-wiring faults need a qualified technician. Repeating a cleaning cycle will not correct an electrical fault and may create additional risk.

When Cleaning Won’t Fix the Problem

Cleaning can resolve taste, smell or flow problems where deposits or contamination are the cause. Where the symptom survives a full clean, the causes below are the ones to look at.

  • Water pooling under the cabinet. If water is pooling near electrical parts, stop using the dispenser and arrange a service. If there is any doubt about electrical exposure, do the same. Where the leak is clearly away from electrical components, note where it appears and have the cause checked. A cracked tank, a loose joint and a worn tap seal each leak from a different point.
  • Cold water sitting at room temperature. Check airflow around the cabinet and the thermostat setting before any sealed-system work is considered.
  • A hot tap still lukewarm after descaling. The element, thermostat and wiring should be tested by a qualified technician.
  • Flow still poor once an air lock is ruled out. The filter or cartridge is one of the first items a technician can check, before moving on to the pump.
  • Noise continuing after vents and coils are clear. Note when it happens. A sound tied to the compressor cycling points somewhere different from one tied to the pump running.

One symptom can have several causes. Have the likely causes tested before replacing a part.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use vinegar in a water dispenser?

Vinegar is used for mineral deposits on some machines, but only use it when the manufacturer names it. It is not a substitute for the manufacturer’s sanitising procedure, and it must never be used alongside bleach.

Can I use bleach in a water dispenser?

Some manufacturers restrict chlorine-based products, because compatibility depends on the materials and internal components of the dispenser. Check what your documentation approves before using any bleach.

How do I know the rinse is complete?

Where the model procedure calls for flushing through each tap, draw water from each one and check for any smell or taste of the cleaner. If the solution remained in the system longer than instructed, follow the manufacturer’s flushing procedure before returning the dispenser to use.

How long can a bottle stay on the dispenser?

Follow the use period stated on the bottle or by the supplier after the bottle is opened and connected.

Do I need to clean a new dispenser before first use?

Follow the commissioning steps in the documentation. These may include rinsing the tank and flushing the taps before the first bottle goes on.

Can the drip tray go in the dishwasher?

Check the model instructions before putting the drip tray in a dishwasher. Some trays are dishwasher-safe; others should be washed by hand.
